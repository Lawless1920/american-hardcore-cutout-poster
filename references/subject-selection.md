# Subject Selection And Photo Preservation

This guide adapts the photo-input and core-subject-routing concepts of **GC Minimal Zine Poster**, by LiamGvchi, under the MIT License. It is adapted for the American Hardcore Cutout Poster visual system and does not import the original minimal-zine art direction. See [`NOTICE.md`](../NOTICE.md).

## Image Role

The customer image is an edit target whenever the user asks to make it into this poster. Preserve its selected hero instead of discarding it for a newly invented scene. Treat supplied inspiration images as reference-only: learn their visual grammar but do not repeat their exact subject, wording, brands, signatures, watermark, or layout.

## Candidate Pass

Inspect the image before picking the hero. Record only observable candidates:

- humans and their visible action or expression;
- animals and their visible action or relationship;
- vehicles, products, tools, or objects with distinctive silhouette/detail;
- an environmental landmark or scene relation when no discrete subject dominates.

Choose one core imageable subject/event using these decision rules, in order:

1. **Relational meaning:** first ask whether two or more foreground elements form the photograph's thesis through their relationship, contrast, balance, or shared presence. If removing one would make the image read as a different photograph, preserve the set before ranking individuals.
2. **Narrative clarity:** prefer the element or subject set that explains what the photograph is about at a glance.
3. **Visual salience:** use scale, contrast, focus, centrality, or framing as supporting evidence—not an automatic reason to discard another comparably important foreground subject.
4. **Distinctive detail:** prefer the element with a recognizable silhouette, expression, pattern, or construction detail that survives a cutout.
5. **Theme potential:** prefer the element that produces a specific 2-4 word emotional title and useful source-derived background crop.

### Collective-Event Override

Before ranking a conspicuous individual, check whether the photograph's core subject is a **collective event**. Treat a team, band, crew, family, or small group as one hero when the people are visibly linked by the same activity, performance, formation, or shared moment, and separating one person would lose the image's meaning. A football team gathered on its pitch, a band performing together, or friends jointly acting in one scene are typical cases.

Do not choose one member merely because they are central, closest to the lens, or holding an object such as a ball, guitar, phone, or drink. When the collective-event override applies, rank the entire group as one candidate, preserve its recognizable members and their spatial relationship, and keep only enough environmental context to explain the event.

### Paired-Foreground Override

Apply this override even when two people are not visibly performing the same action. Treat two or three foreground people as one paired subject set when their juxtaposition is the reason the image works: they carry comparable visual weight, create a deliberate near/far or left/right counterpoint, or their distinct silhouettes, clothes, gaze directions, and positions together define the scene. A posed pair at an event, two foreground friends with different orientations, or an intimate two-person street image can qualify.

Do not reduce such a pair to the person with the clearer face, sharper focus, more frontal pose, higher contrast, central placement, or a visible credential/prop. Those are secondary salience cues only. When the override applies, preserve both people at usable scale and draw one shared line-cut contour around the pair; the other foreground person is not mere environmental context.

When two candidates tie after both overrides have been considered, keep their relationship only if splitting it would destroy the photograph's meaning. Otherwise choose the most salient candidate and let the other remain as context. Do not preserve multiple unrelated people/objects merely because they appear in the frame.

For a scene without a clear person, animal, or object, select the strongest photographic event—such as a landmark, weather formation, roadway, architectural shape, or repeated texture—and make it the hero. Do not invent a protagonist.

## Preservation Invariants

For the selected hero, explicitly preserve the applicable visible facts:

- a person's identity, facial structure, apparent age, hair, clothing silhouette, pose, and expression;
- an animal's species, markings, body shape, and pose;
- a vehicle/product/object's geometry, count, defining parts, color, and markings;
- a landmark/scene's essential silhouette, terrain, scale relationship, and weather/time cues.

The poster may crop, scale, cut around, screen-print, and surround the hero. It may not replace, merge, or hallucinate its defining identity.

## Theme And Title

Extract one emotional temperature from the hero and visible situation: velocity, defiance, solitude, pressure, escape, heat, frost, late-night energy, noise, or stillness. Turn that into a fresh 2-4 word English phrase, avoiding direct description when a more evocative phrase fits. The phrase must be short enough for individually assembled ransom letters and must be rendered exactly.
